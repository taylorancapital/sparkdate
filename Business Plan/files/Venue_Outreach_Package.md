# Venue Outreach Package — SparkDate Philadelphia

**Goal:** Lock in 8-15 free venue partnerships in Months 1-3 (Phase 1)
**Pitch:** "Free platform. We bring 25-40 qualified, paying customers to your slow nights. They run separate tabs off your regular menu and you keep 100% of it — roughly $500-$2,000 depending on your check average."

---

## F&B ESTIMATE — read before quoting any number

**There is no measured F&B figure.** Attendees open separate tabs, so the spend
lands in the venue's POS and never in ours; we see ticket revenue only. No venue
has ever reported a total back. Every figure below is a **projection**, and the
only grounded input is Tellus at **$18-22 a head** (about three drinks, plus tip
— and tip is staff money, not the owner's F&B line).

Per-head spend is a property of **the venue**, not of SparkDate. A dive bar and
Parc cannot produce the same number, which is why one fixed figure must never be
quoted to every venue.

| Venue tier | Avg check / head | 25 guests | 30 guests | 40 guests |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Casual / dive (Tellus, Good Good) | ~$20 | $500 | $600 | $800 |
| Mid / gastropub | ~$35 | $875 | $1,050 | $1,400 |
| Upscale cocktail (Tier 1 target) | ~$50 | $1,250 | $1,500 | $2,000 |

**Range: $500-$2,000. Typical mid room at 30 guests: ~$1,050.**

In outbound copy, hand the multiplier to the operator — they know their own check
average, and at a $50 room it lands them above anything we would have asserted.
Never write "most venues see." Working: `reports/VENUE_PITCH_FACT_AUDIT_2026-09-01.md`.

**RETAINED as the standing estimate — Taylor, 2026-09-02.** The mid tier (~$35/head, **~$1,050 at 30 guests**) reads realistic and is the default for planning and for any figure quoted internally. Asking a partner venue for an actual tab total was considered and **declined** — so this stays a projection **by choice, not by oversight.** Do not re-open it as a measurement gap, and do not upgrade it to an observation without new data actually arriving.

## TARGET VENUE CRITERIA — REVISED 2026-09-02: beer gardens and large outdoor

**This replaces the cocktail-bar targeting below it.** The original Tier 1 was
"cocktail-forward, design-conscious, Instagram-able" at a $25-50 check. That was
never what was actually wanted — the first venues Taylor named were Walnut Garden
and Stratus, both outdoor. The list and the intent had drifted apart.

### Why this fits the format better, not just the preference

- **The run of show needs room.** Table rounds, then open mingling, then 1-on-1s.
  Picnic-table seating with open space between is close to purpose-built for that.
  A single long table (Vernick Wine seats 28 that way) or a narrow cocktail bar is not.
- **It is easier to walk in alone.** The Good Good debrief landed on this as the
  live hypothesis — an outside operator independently reported hotel lobbies
  outperforming bars, and the reason given was not ambience but whether arriving
  alone is comfortable. An open-air garden is the easiest room in the city to
  walk into alone.
- **Headroom.** Uptown is 18,000 sq ft and 700 standing; Independence is 20,000 sq
  ft. A 40-person event does not strain them, so nobody is doing us a favour by
  fitting us in.

### Tier 1 — Ideal

- **Space:** large outdoor, picnic-table or communal seating, room to move between
  a seated phase and a standing phase
- **Capacity:** 100+ standing. We bring 25-40; the room should not feel tight
- **Covered or indoor fallback: REQUIRED.** See the seasonality note below — this
  is the difference between a partner and an eight-week partner
- **Avg check:** $18-28/person. Casual tier. Lower per head than the old target
  band, deliberately — see the F&B note
- **Slow nights:** Monday-Wednesday, but **check the hours first.** Independence
  is closed Mon-Tue, so "your slow Tuesday" is a non-sequitur there
- **Neighborhoods:** Delaware waterfront (Columbus Blvd), Fishtown/Kensington,
  Center City, Old City, Manayunk

### Tier 2 — Strong fit

Brewery taprooms with a yard, rooftops with real square footage, food halls with
outdoor seating, social clubs with grounds.

### AVOID

- Pure nightclubs and sports bars (unchanged — too loud for conversation)
- **Seasonal-only gardens with no covered space, for anything past October**
- Anywhere already busy Mon-Wed
- Roving/pop-up gardens with no fixed address (Parks on Tap) — no operator to
  build a relationship with

## ⚠ SEASONALITY — the constraint that governs this whole list

**Written 2026-09-02. Philadelphia outdoor season ends in late October.** A
beer-garden-first strategy has roughly **eight weeks** of runway this year.

This does not kill the pivot, it orders it:

1. **Year-round and covered venues first** — they are partners in November too.
   Cherry Street Pier states year-round hours. Frankford Hall, Evil Genius and
   Uptown all have covered or indoor space.
2. **Purely seasonal gardens are a spring conversation.** Independence, Bok Bar
   and Morgan's Pier are worth contacting **now to book for May**, not to fill an
   October Tuesday. Pitch them as next season's recurring slot.

Do not send an "your slow Tuesday" email to an uncovered garden in November.

---

## F&B — what this pivot does to the estimate

Beer gardens sit in the **casual tier, ~$20-25 a head**, not the $35 mid tier.
Read against the F&B ESTIMATE table above, 30 guests is **$600-750**, not $1,050.

**But the headcount ceiling moves, and it moves further.** These rooms hold
hundreds. At a 60-person event — well within Uptown's 700 or Independence's
20,000 sq ft, and impossible at Vernick — $22 a head is **~$1,320**.

So the trade is real and it is favourable: **lower spend per head, far more heads
available.** Quote it the same way regardless — hand the operator their own check
average and let them multiply. Never assert a figure.

## OBJECTION HANDLING — THE TOP 7

### "We don't host third-party events."
*"This isn't a third-party event in the traditional sense — there's no rental fee, no production costs to you, and no exclusive buyout. We promote your space on a slow night, drive 25-40 paying guests, and they buy from your normal menu at normal prices. You keep 100% of food and beverage revenue. Worst case, you have a quiet Tuesday made busier."*

### "What's the catch? Why is this free?"
*"We make money from the consumers — they pay for tickets and a small subscription. Our economics work because we deliver high-quality groups, not because we charge venues. Charging venues is what kills similar models. We're betting that if we make this great for you, you'll keep the slot, recommend us to other venues, and eventually upgrade to a paid tier when we add value beyond foot traffic."*

### "What if your people are weird / underdressed / loud?"
*"Every attendee is identity-verified and pre-screened. We host on-site for the first 30 minutes. Your bartenders and managers retain full discretion to refuse service to anyone who isn't a fit — voucher or no voucher. Our brand depends on your brand. We can't afford to send the wrong crowd."*

### "What kind of crowd?"
*"Philadelphia young professionals, ages 25-34, household incomes $60K-$100K+, college-educated, gender balanced. Think: Hinge users who got tired of Hinge. We curate aggressively — most events skew 50%+ female, which is unusual and means more balanced spending."*

### "Who handles the alcohol liability?"
*"You do — same as any normal night. We don't sell or distribute alcohol. We don't issue drink vouchers in Pennsylvania (PA prohibits free drink promos at licensed venues). Attendees buy directly from your bar at your normal prices. We provide host insurance naming you as additional insured. You retain all your normal protections."*

### "How often?"
*"We start with one night per month. If both sides are happy after two events, we move to weekly or bi-weekly on a recurring slot. We never want to be on your busy nights — only the ones where we're additive."*

### "What do we have to do?"
*"Almost nothing. Confirm a date and time. Brief your team that we're coming (we send a one-pager). Set aside a corner or back room for our intro circle if possible. That's it. We handle promotion, RSVPs, check-in, host facilitation. You pour drinks and serve food."*
