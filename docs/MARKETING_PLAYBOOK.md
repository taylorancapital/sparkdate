# SparkDate Marketing Playbook — Lancaster Launch

**Goal:** pack the **Founders Mixer (Wed Jun 24, 6:30 PM, American Bar & Grill,
Lancaster)** and build organic traffic so you stop depending on a single paid
Facebook channel.

**Where you are today:** 75 site visitors, ~$14 spent on a Facebook carousel,
all sales via Eventbrite. The bottleneck isn't the product — it's **one channel,
and it's paid.** Everything below adds free/organic channels and fixes the ad.

> **The one thing that matters right now:** get bodies in the room on Jun 24.
> A full, high-energy first event is the asset that makes every future
> venue pitch, press mention, and ticket sale easier. If you have to comp or
> discount a few tickets to friends to seed the room, do it — a half-empty
> first event costs you far more than the free tickets.

## 3. Fix the Facebook ad (your carousel is the problem)

Carousels are built for retargeting warm audiences, not cold local discovery.
For a cold local audience, **one strong image + one clear CTA** wins.

**New creative (single image or 15-sec video):**
- **Visual:** the venue or a warm "people laughing at a bar" shot. Real > stock.
- **Headline:** `Singles Night in Lancaster — Wed Jun 24`
- **Primary text:** `Skip the endless texting. Meet 12+ single people face-to-face in one fun night at American Bar & Grill. Early-bird tickets $18.`
- **CTA button:** `Get Tickets`
- **Destination:** your native event page with `?utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=paid` so GA4 attributes it.

**Targeting:** Lancaster PA + 25-mi radius · ages 25–45 · all genders (watch your
mix and adjust). Interests: dating, singles, nightlife, local events.

**Budget:** concentrate it. **$7–10/day for the 6–7 days before the event**
beats the same money spread thin over weeks. You want frequency in the final
stretch when intent is highest.

**Set up retargeting for next time:** make sure the Meta Pixel is on the site
(it likely is, given GA is). Then *this* event's visitors become a warm audience
you can hit with the carousel for the *next* event — that's where carousels work.

## 4. Ticket hubs — keep it simple

You don't need to abandon Eventbrite — its discovery (people browsing
"Lancaster singles party") is a real free funnel. Strategy:

- **Eventbrite = discovery + social proof.** Keep listing there; optimize the
  title (`Singles Night Lancaster — Meet 12+ People | SparkDate`) and add photos.
- **Your site = primary conversion + lower fees + owns the customer.** Drive ad
  and organic traffic to `sparkdate.date`, where the buyer gets the full
  account + chemistry profile automatically (Eventbrite buyers need the manual
  /admin Enroll step — see `docs/EVENTBRITE_ENROLLMENT.md`).
- **Meetup** doubles as a ticket/RSVP hub *and* a recurring discovery channel —
  worth the ~$24/mo organizer fee once you're running monthly events.

Don't sink time into a dozen ticketing platforms right now. More **discovery
listings** (§1) and **organic posts** (§2) will move the needle far more than a
new checkout platform.

## 6. The crowd ↔ venue chicken-and-egg

You need crowds to land venues and venues to draw crowds. Break it like this:

- **Pitch slow nights.** Tue–Thu early evening. A Wednesday at American Bar &
  Grill is perfect — you're *filling their dead night*, which is a favor to them.
- **Lead with their upside, not your headcount.** "I bring 20–40 singles who buy
  food and drinks on a slow Wednesday" beats quoting attendance you don't have.
- **Offer a minimum F&B guarantee** instead of asking for a free room — it's how
  small organizers get a yes. Low risk for them.
- **After Jun 24, you have proof.** Photos, a headcount, testimonials. The next
  venue conversation flips from "trust me" to "here's what happened last time."
  Take photos and a short video on the 24th — that's your sales kit.
