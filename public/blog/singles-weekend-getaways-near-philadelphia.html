Weekend getaways for singles near Philadelphia

Everything worth escaping to within about two hours of the city — and the one thing that decides whether a "singles trip" is actually social or just a solo trip with extra steps.

Philadelphia is quietly one of the best-positioned cities in the country for a weekend escape. Mountains to the north, beaches to the east, farm country and small-town main streets to the west — all close enough that you can leave after work on Friday and be somewhere that feels genuinely different before dinner.

But if you're single, there's a catch nobody warns you about: most "getaways" are built for couples. The cabin sleeps two. The spa package is priced for pairs. The winery tour seats you across from someone else's anniversary. Going alone is doable — plenty of people love solo travel — but if part of the point is meeting someone, the standard weekend trip is set up against you.

Here's the honest guide: where to go, and how to make it social.

The Poconos: cabin country, 90 minutes north

The classic Philly escape for a reason. Two hours or less puts you in real mountains with lakes, waterfall hikes (Bushkill Falls, Ricketts Glen a bit farther), and the kind of cabin-with-a-fireplace weekend that fills your camera roll in October and your soul in January.

The singles catch: cabins are isolating by design. A romantic cabin for one is called a retreat; for it to be a social weekend, you need a group — which is why a shared lodge with eight-to-twelve people you'd actually want to meet is a completely different product than a couples' cabin. Board games, shared meals, a firepit that does the introductions for you.

Best for

Fall foliage, winter fireplaces, unplugging

Book Friday–Sunday, arrive before dark, and pick a place with one big common space — that's where the weekend actually happens.

Cape May & the Shore: the walkable beach weekend

Skip the party-shore chaos and aim for Cape May: a Victorian beach town that's walkable end to end, full of porches, wine bars, and sunset spots at the point. It's the rare shore town where being an adult without kids in tow feels like the default rather than the exception. Asbury Park, farther north, trades the Victorian charm for live music and a boardwalk scene that skews single and thirty-something.

Brandywine Valley & Lancaster: slow weekends, close by

Under an hour from the city: Longwood Gardens, chateau-style wineries, small-town main streets (West Chester, Kennett Square, Lititz), and enough B&Bs to fill a season of weekends. This is the "reset" trip — the one you take when you're tired, not when you're feeling adventurous. If you're Lancaster-based, this is your backyard already; our Lancaster date-night guide covers the town side of it.

The spa weekend: yes, you can go single

Spa resorts within reach of Philly (the Poconos again, plus a handful in the Brandywine area) are the most couples-coded getaway of all — and also the one where the couples-coding matters least. Treatments are solo by nature. The awkward part is dinner. A spa weekend built for a group of singles flips that: pools and treatments by day, a shared table and low-key mingling by night. It's the getaway for people whose idea of connection starts at "relaxed," not "activity schedule."

The difference between a solo trip and a singles trip is one thing: whether anyone is responsible for the introductions. Scenery doesn't introduce people. Hosts do.

What actually makes a singles getaway work

We've hosted enough mixers to know the mechanics of a good room, and a good singles trip runs on the same three things — just stretched across a weekend instead of an evening:

1

A curated group, not an open invite

Twelve compatible people beat forty random ones. The same reason our mixers cap the room applies double when you're sharing a lodge.

2

Structure at the start, freedom after

One hosted dinner or activity on night one, so everyone has met everyone. After that, the weekend runs itself — the hard part (the cold start) is already done.

3

A shared space that does the work

Firepit, big kitchen table, pool deck — one gravity point where the group naturally re-collects. Hotels with private everything kill the momentum a group builds.
