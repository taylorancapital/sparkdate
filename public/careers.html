Run the room.
Get paid for the night.

SparkDate puts 20–30 singles in a good bar for one evening and makes sure they actually talk to each other. That last part is the job. We're looking for Event Hosts in ten US cities.

The job

You run a SparkDate mixer from doors to last call. You are the reason a room of strangers turns into a room of conversations, and the reason nobody stands alone by the bar looking at their phone.

This is not bartending and it is not promo work. Nobody is asking you to hand out flyers or hit a sales target. You are hosting an evening — greeting people by name, reading who has not spoken to anyone yet, and making the introduction they were not going to make themselves.

  • Greet and check in every guest as they arrive, on your phone
  • Run the icebreaker that opens the night
  • Watch the room and make introductions — especially to anyone drifting
  • Keep the evening on its rails: drinks, icebreaker, conversations, in that order
  • Handle the small stuff — a no-show, a late arrival, a guest who needs a hand
  • Send us the real attendance count and anything worth knowing afterwards

A night, end to end

6:00 PM

Arrive early

Meet the venue contact, confirm the space and the tab, set up. You are there before anyone else and you leave after they do.

6:30 PM

Doors

Check guests in, learn names fast, get drinks moving. First fifteen minutes set the temperature of the whole night.

7:00 PM

Icebreaker

You run it. It is short, it is not humiliating, and it exists so that nobody has to invent an opening line cold.

7:20 PM

The actual evening

Conversations, and you working the room. This is the part that makes the difference between an event people remember and a party they left early.

9:00 PM

Close out

Thank people, settle up with the venue, send us the headcount and anything we should know for next time.

You'd be good at this if

  • You are the person who introduces two friends who have not met
  • You can tell, across a room, who is having a bad time — and fix it without making it obvious
  • You are warm before you are funny, and you are genuinely interested in people
  • You are dependable about evenings. An event happens whether or not you feel like it
  • You know your city's bars, or you are willing to go find the good ones
  • You can stay entirely sober and entirely social for four hours

Hospitality, events, teaching, bartending, retail, theatre and front-desk backgrounds all translate well. So does none of the above — we care much more about how you are in a room than what is on your CV.
