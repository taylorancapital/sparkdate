# Review — PR #400 and #401 (2026-09-02)

**This review changes no site code and no ad.** It adds this file only.

It reviews two merged PRs: `reports/META_ADS_REVIEW_2026-09-02.md` (#400, the
lifetime ad history plus `scripts/meta-ads-review.js`) and
`reports/AD_LEVER_WOMEN_2026-09-02.md` (#401, the plan drawn from it).

**What I checked.** Every arithmetic partition in both reports, reconstructed
from their own appendix tables; every cross-reference to another report, to
Firestore code, and to memory; and `scripts/meta-ads-review.js` line by line
against the claims it is supposed to make re-runnable. I did **not** re-pull the
Meta API — every number below is the reports' own, checked against itself.

---

## The short version

**The arithmetic is clean and the §1 leak finding holds.** Every partition
reconciles to the cent. The strongest claim in either report — that $241.74 of
the $871.71 spent in women-targeted sets reached men, including $39.60 of the
$61.76 behind a women-only 2-for-1 offer — survives review, and its mechanism is
correctly labelled inferred rather than proven.

**Two conclusions are drawn from a metric the same report calls broken.** The
2-for-1 verdict quotes 880 women's landing-page views when 460 of them are on
ads that cannot report a purchase at all. And the creative verdict ("proof beats
the offer") rests on two purchases that are also the report's evidence for
"retargeting beats prospecting" — the same two ads, counted twice.

**The scorecard cannot score the top action item.** `npm run ads:review` never
requests `tracking_specs`. Next week's run will not tell you whether the pixel
got attached.

## FINDING 1 — CONFIRMED. The 2-for-1 verdict rests on half the evidence it cites

views, zero women's purchases**. #401 step 4 repeats it as the reason to demote
the offer: *"as a prospecting headline it has bought 880 women's landing-page
views and never a seat."*

I reconstructed the bucket from Appendix A. It reconciles exactly — 12 ads,
$351.75 of spend, 880 women's LPV — so the classification is recoverable. Of
those 880:

| 2-for-1 ad | Women's LPV | Pixel in tracking? |
|---|---:|---|

| MC Women - Video Ad (Traffic) | 178 | **no** (#400 §5) |
| Landingpage - Event 3 Women | 115 | yes |
| Tellus … Women Ad | 97 | yes |

| Loxleys \| female \| prime video | 42 | yes |
| Landingpage - Event 3 Women USA | 32 | yes |

**52% of the evidence for the 2-for-1's zero comes from two ads #400 §5 says
cannot count a purchase.** #400 applied that caveat correctly in §2 ("the 0 in

**The conclusion probably survives.** 420 measurable women's landing-page views
with zero purchases is still a real zero, and memory `social-proof-pulls-women`
reaches the same verdict independently. But say 420, not 880 — and note that
demoting the 2-for-1 is a decision made on ~$180 of measurable spend, not $352.

## FINDING 2 — CONFIRMED. `npm run ads:review` cannot verify action item #1

Both reports open with the same first move: attach the pixel to the two live
Marion Court Traffic ads, because *"nothing below can be scored"* until it lands.

`scripts/meta-ads-review.js` never asks for it. `AD_FIELDS` (lines 91–96)
requests campaign, adset, targeting and creative — **not `tracking_specs`, not
`promoted_object`.** A grep of the file returns zero hits for either. The §5
finding was made by hand ("checked ad by ad on 2026-09-02"), and #400 §5 is the
only record of which five ads are affected.

So the tool billed as the weekly scorecard — #400 DECISION #2 says *"`npm run
ads:review` prints 'women share of spend' beside each set's target"*, which it
does — is blind to the one change everything else waits on.

**Fix, roughly one line plus a column:** add `tracking_specs` to `AD_FIELDS` and
print a "pixel in tracking" column in the per-ad table. `scripts/meta-create-lx-prime-ads.js`
is the only script in the repo that touches the field today and shows the shape.

## FINDING 3 — CONFIRMED. "No signal for Meta to learn from" is wrong, and #400 contradicts itself

#400's opening paragraph: the unpixeled Traffic ads *"count a landing-page view
and nothing after it: no cart, no checkout, no purchase, **and no signal for Meta
to learn from**."*

#400 §5, third bullet, says the opposite and is correct: *"The pixel fires for
every visitor whichever ad sent them."* Meta receives the events. What is missing
is **per-ad attribution in reporting**, not the signal itself.

There is a second half to this that neither report states. `tracking_specs` sets
what an ad *reports*; delivery optimisation is the ad set's `optimization_goal`,
and both Marion Court Traffic sets optimise `LINK_CLICKS`. **Attaching the pixel
will let those ads count a purchase. It will not change who Meta shows them to.**

That does not make action item #1 wrong — it is still the prerequisite for
scoring anything. It means it should be sold as a **measurement fix with no
expected lift**, so that a flat week afterwards is not read as failure. Changing
delivery is a different edit: a conversion optimisation goal with a
`promoted_object`, which is what the Sales-objective sets already do.

## FINDING 4 — CONFIRMED. "Proof beats the offer" and "retargeting beats prospecting" are the same two data points

#400 §3 gives the social-proof theme 2 of the 3 women's purchases on $304.16.
The very next paragraph gives retargeting 4 of 6 purchases on $307. Those are
nearly the same dollars: both women's purchases in the social-proof bucket sit in
**Good Good Retargeting** and **Tellus Retargeting** — both Sales objective, both
pixeled, both warm audiences, both Facebook placement.

So "proof converts women", "retargeting converts women", "Sales objective
converts women" and "Facebook converts women" (#401's placement table) are four
readings of the same two purchases. Theme is collinear with funnel stage, and
n = 2.

The creative recommendation may still be right — memory `social-proof-pulls-women`
records Taylor's own independent read from 08-25, four purchases on the Tellus
social-proof creative in a 59%-female week. That is the stronger evidence.
§3's table is not evidence for theme independent of stage, and should not be
quoted as if it were.

## FINDING 5 — CONFIRMED. §3's theme classification is not reproducible, and one ad is misfiled

The script does not classify themes; #400 §3 says only *"classified from the
primary text"*. There is no ad→theme mapping in either report, so the table
driving the biggest creative decision cannot be audited without redoing the
classification by hand — which is what this review had to do.

Doing it surfaced one error. The twelfth 2-for-1 ad is **`Loxleys | female |
prime video`**, whose primary text leads *"Not an app. Not speed dating. A room
of people who actually decided to show up."* The 2-for-1 is its **last** line.
§3's stated method is "by the copy's lead line", so by its own rule this ad is
positioning, not offer.

It matters because that is the exact ad #401 calls the cheapest click in the
account ($0.10–$0.11) and recommends weighting budget toward. It is currently
counted as evidence against the theme it is not leading with.

**Fix:** publish the mapping — one `theme` field per ad in the JSON the script
already writes, or an appendix column.

## FINDING 6 — CONFIRMED. Meta's gender rows do not reconcile with Meta's own totals

Appendix A, `Loxleys | female | prime video`: **"women share of LPV 105.00%."**

The per-ad table shows why — the female breakdown row and the all row are
identical on spend, impressions, reach and clicks, and disagree on landing-page
views:

| Gender | Spend | Impr | Reach | Link clicks | LPV |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| female | $4.76 | 599 | 489 | 46 | **42** |
| **all** | $4.76 | 599 | 489 | 46 | **40** |

The script runs two separate queries — `date_preset: maximum` with and without
`breakdowns: gender` (lines 232–233) — and Meta returns different conversion
counts for each. Every "cost per women's LPV" in both reports comes from the
breakdown query; every `$/LPV` in Appendix A comes from the total. They are not
the same denominator.

Here it is 5% on a 40-LPV ad. **The size and direction of the error on the large
ads is unknown**, and 105% went to print unremarked.

**Fix:** have the script sum the gender rows and compare to the total per ad,
printing a warning above some threshold. It is a few lines and it turns an
invisible discrepancy into a visible one.

## FINDING 7 — CONFIRMED. The placement table is all genders; the sentence under it says "a woman's"

#401 states the limitation twice and correctly — Meta refused the
placement×gender combination (`(#100) Current combination of data breakdown
columns … is invalid`), and "What I did not verify" repeats it. Then the prose
directly under the table reads: *"Instagram delivers **a woman's** landing-page
view at half Facebook's price."*

That table is all genders, all ads. The claim it can support is "Instagram
delivers a landing-page view at half Facebook's price."

The **per-ad** table below it is genuinely women-only (those sets are women-only)
and does support the weaker, sufficient claim: the women's prime ads already run
almost entirely on Instagram and get clicks at $0.10–$0.24. That is enough for
DECISION #3 without the overclaim.

The Facebook-purchases half (5 vs 1) is Finding 4 again: Facebook holds the
purchases because the Sales retargeting ads serve there. Placement is confounded
with funnel stage, so "prospect on IG, retarget on both" describes what already
happened rather than testing placement.

## FINDING 8 — CONFIRMED, minor. An ad named "…Sale Obj Women" runs in the "…All Genders" set

Appendix A, row 15: ad **"Campaign 1 Event 4 Good Good Campaign-Sale Obj Women"**
sits in ad set **"Campaign 1 Event 4 Good Good Campaign-Sale Obj All Genders"**,
targeting `all 18-65`, $44.77, 40.23% of spend to women.

Not a leak — its copy is the "28 people came" social proof, not the women-only
2-for-1, so nothing restricted ran where it should not have. But it is the exact
naming trap #400 §1 is about, and the next person reading the account will
mis-file it. Rename the ad.

## FINDING 9 — PLAUSIBLE. The site-visitor audience has an unchecked precondition

#401 step 2 adds "Visited but did not order tickets" to the Marion Court
retargeting set. The audience displays at Meta's **≥ 20** floor. #401 argues the
pixel's 2,837 weekly PageViews mean it fills at that rate.

PageViews are not unique matched Facebook users, and browser-level blocking cuts
match rates hard. The audience may be genuinely small.

**This is low-risk either way** — multiple custom audiences in one ad set are a
union, not an intersection, so adding it cannot narrow delivery. The failure mode
is that it adds nothing and the retargeting frequency problem stays. Read
`approximate_count_lower_bound` and `delivery_status` on that audience first; it
is one API call, and the review script could print it beside each set.

- **`thruplay` is always 0.** `video_thruplay_watched_actions` is a top-level

  $558.29 + $500.07 + $139.41 = $1,197.77 and 23 + 12 + 5 = 40 ads. §3 by theme:
  the five buckets also sum to $1,197.77 and 40 ads, and their women's LPV sums
  to 1,627 — the same figure as §2's three buckets and the headline. $786.45 /
  1,627 = $0.483 ✓. Purchases 3 women / 3 men ✓ in both cuts. Two independent
  partitions of forty ads agreeing to the cent is a good sign about the pull.
- **Every cross-reference I spot-checked is real and precise**, including
  `api/cron-send-emails.js:491` — that line is exactly where a registration with
  no `userId` is skipped, which is why a comped +1 never gets the profile prompt
  — and the GA4 09-01 figures ($0.102/session on `/lp`, $2.42 on `/event`,
  $3.30 on Eventbrite-listing traffic).
- **The §1 leak finding holds and is the strongest thing in either report.**
  $241.74 of $871.71 (28%), the Good Good 2-for-1 row at $39.60 of $61.76 to men,
  the eleven-set table summing to $232.01 of that $241.74. The mechanism is
  labelled **NOT VERIFIED** with the exact call that would settle it

- **The "What I did not verify" sections are specific and honest**, and #401's
  data note reproduces the literal Meta error that blocked the placement×gender
  split. Nothing important is hidden.

## What this means for the three questions Taylor asked

**"The Traffic campaigns appear functional."** They are functional at buying
clicks and unmeasurable at everything after — the two live Marion Court Traffic
ads are two of the five unpixeled ads. Until Finding 2's fix lands, "functional"
is not a falsifiable statement about them.

**"Spend where there is conversion."** The reports' own numbers point at
Sales-objective retargeting: $500.07 → 3 women's purchases; Traffic $558.29 → 0,
with the two biggest women's Traffic ads unable to count one. Yet #400 DECISION
#7 and #401 step 3 both push prospecting budget toward Traffic video, justified
on cost-per-landing-page-view — the metric #400 itself says "is not a seat."

There is a good defence neither report makes: **prospecting Traffic is what fills
the retargeting pool, and 4 of 6 purchases are retargeting.** If that is the
rationale, say so, because it changes the scoring: judge Traffic by pool growth
and by downstream retargeting purchases, never by its own purchase count. Judged
on its own purchases, Traffic will read as a failure forever.

**"One or two action items."** Two, and they are already #1 and #2 in #401:

1. **Pixel on the two live Marion Court Traffic ads.** Expect measurement, not
   lift (Finding 3).
2. **Site-visitor audience into the Marion Court retargeting set** — after the
   one API call that confirms it is not empty (Finding 9).

Plus one that costs nothing and is not on either list: **add `tracking_specs` to
`meta-ads-review.js`** so #1 can be confirmed next week rather than re-checked by
hand (Finding 2).

The budget weighting (#401 step 6, Loxleys 70/30) is a third real item but it is
a Loxleys decision on a 09-22 event, and HANDOFF holds it for its own chat.

**Timing.** HANDOFF records both Marion Court campaigns re-entering learning on
09-02 and says do not tune either before **2026-09-08 16:30**. The pixel and
audience edits are the stated exception. Make them once.

## What I did not verify

- **I did not re-pull the Meta API.** Every figure here is the reports' own,
  checked against itself. If the pull was wrong, this review inherits it.
- **Whether the 420 measurable 2-for-1 women's LPV is the right denominator.**
  I classified pixel coverage from #400 §5's list of five ads; ads outside that
  list are assumed pixeled on the report's word.
- **The gender-reconciliation error on any ad but Loxleys female.** I found the
  one that crossed 100% and made it visible. I did not sum gender rows against
  totals for the other 39 — the appendix has the data to do it.
- **Meta's `tracking_specs`-does-not-change-delivery claim** is from the
  Marketing API's documented model, not from an experiment on this account.
- **The theme classification for the four buckets other than 2-for-1.** I
  reconciled that one exactly; the others I sampled and could not reproduce to
  the cent, which is Finding 5.
